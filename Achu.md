# Achu Recipe (Cameroonian Yellow Soup and Taro)

## 1. Introduction

Achu is a traditional Cameroonian dish, especially popular in the Northwest and Southwest Regions. It consists of pounded cocoyam (taro) served with a yellow soup made from palm oil, limestone water, and traditional spices. It is commonly accompanied by meat, cow skin (canda), tripe, or fish.

## 2. Preparation Time

- **Preparation time:** 30 minutes
- **Cooking time:** 1–2 hours
- **Total time:** Approximately 2 hours
- **Servings:** 4–6 people

## 3. Ingredients

### A. For the Achu (Pounded Taro)

- 2 kg cocoyam (taro)
- Water for boiling

### B. For the Yellow Soup

- 250 ml red palm oil
- 1–2 teaspoons traditional achu spice (yellow soup spice blend)
- 1/2 teaspoon ground country onion, if available
- 1/2 teaspoon ground ginger, optional
- 1–2 teaspoons limestone water (food-grade limewater), prepared according to local practice
- Salt to taste
- Warm water as needed

**Note:** Traditional achu soup recipes vary by family and region. Use food-grade ingredients only. Do not use caustic lime or construction lime.

### C. For the Meat

- 1 kg assorted meat, such as beef, tripe, cow skin (canda), or smoked fish
- 1 medium onion, optional
- Salt to taste
- 1 teaspoon seasoning powder, optional
- Water for cooking

## 4. Instructions

### Step 1: Prepare the Meat

1. Wash the meat thoroughly using safe, clean water.
2. Cut it into manageable pieces.
3. Place it in a pot and add salt, onion, and seasoning if desired.
4. Add enough water to cook the meat.
5. Cover and simmer until tender. Tougher cuts, such as tripe and cow skin, may require longer cooking.
6. Keep the meat stock for serving or for another dish.

### Step 2: Cook the Cocoyam

1. Wash the cocoyam thoroughly to remove dirt.
2. Place it in a large pot and cover with water.
3. Boil until completely soft. A fork or knife should pass through easily.
4. Drain the water and allow the cocoyam to cool slightly.
5. Peel off the skin carefully while the cocoyam is still warm enough to handle safely.

### Step 3: Pound the Achu

1. Place the peeled cocoyam in a mortar.
2. Pound until it becomes smooth, elastic, and free of lumps.
3. If the mixture is too thick, add a small amount of hot water and continue pounding.
4. Knead the mixture until it forms a smooth, stretchy dough.

**Alternative method:** Use a heavy-duty food processor suitable for thick, sticky mixtures. Process the cooked cocoyam in small batches until smooth, adding very little hot water if necessary.

### Step 4: Prepare the Yellow Soup

1. Pour the red palm oil into a clean bowl.
2. Gradually add warm water while stirring continuously to help the oil emulsify.
3. Add the achu spice and country onion, if using.
4. Add the food-grade limestone water gradually, following a trusted traditional recipe. Stir well.
5. Continue mixing until the soup becomes creamy and develops its characteristic yellow colour.
6. Add salt to taste.
7. Adjust the consistency with small amounts of warm water as needed.

**Important:** Limestone water is traditionally used in some achu recipes to help the soup emulsify. Its concentration matters. Do not add dry lime powder directly or use an unknown chemical preparation. If you are unfamiliar with preparing food-grade limewater, ask an experienced achu cook for the appropriate preparation and quantity.

### Step 5: Assemble and Serve

1. Shape the pounded cocoyam into individual portions.
2. Place each portion in a serving bowl.
3. Pour the yellow soup over or beside the achu.
4. Arrange the cooked meat, cow skin, tripe, or fish alongside it.
5. Serve warm.

## 5. Tips for the Best Achu

- **Choose good cocoyam:** Mature, starchy cocoyam usually produces a smoother, more elastic achu.
- **Cook thoroughly:** Undercooked taro can irritate the mouth and throat. Cook it completely before pounding.
- **Pound while warm:** Warm cocoyam is generally easier to pound into a smooth dough.
- **Mix the soup patiently:** Add water gradually while stirring to help create a creamy emulsion.
- **Use traditional spices:** A locally prepared achu spice blend gives the soup its distinctive flavour.
- **Adjust carefully:** Too much water can make the soup thin, while too much oil can make it greasy.

## 6. Serving Suggestions

Achu is traditionally served with:
- Beef
- Tripe
- Cow skin (canda)
- Smoked or fresh fish
- Other meats prepared according to personal preference

## 7. Storage

- Refrigerate leftover cooked meat and soup promptly in covered containers.
- Store pounded cocoyam separately and refrigerate it promptly.
- Reheat leftovers thoroughly before eating.
- Avoid leaving cooked food at room temperature for extended periods.

## 8. Conclusion

Achu is a beloved traditional Cameroonian dish known for its smooth, stretchy pounded cocoyam and rich yellow soup. With properly cooked cocoyam, traditional spices, and carefully prepared soup, you can enjoy this classic dish at home.
