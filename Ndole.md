# Traditional Cameroonian Ndolé Recipe

## 1. Introduction

Ndolé is one of Cameroon's most popular traditional dishes. It is prepared with bitter leaves, groundnuts (peanuts), and meat, fish, or seafood. It is especially associated with the coastal region of Cameroon.

The dish is known for its rich groundnut sauce and distinctive bitter flavour. It is commonly served with boiled plantains, bobolo, miondo, or other traditional accompaniments.

## 2. Preparation Time

- **Preparation time:** 45–60 minutes
- **Cooking time:** 1–1½ hours
- **Total time:** Approximately 2 hours
- **Servings:** 4–6 people

## 3. Ingredients

### A. For the Ndolé

- 500 g bitter leaves (fresh or prepared frozen ndolé leaves)
- 500 g raw groundnuts (peanuts)
- 500 g beef
- 250 g smoked fish or dried fish
- 250 g prawns or shrimp (optional)
- 200 g cow skin (canda), optional
- 2 medium onions
- 3 cloves garlic
- 1 piece of ginger, approximately 2–3 cm
- 150–250 ml red palm oil
- 2 seasoning cubes, optional
- Salt to taste
- Fresh hot pepper, to taste
- Water as needed

### B. For Additional Flavour

- 2–3 tablespoons ground crayfish
- 1 teaspoon ground white pepper, optional
- 1 teaspoon country onion, optional

*Note: Ingredients and quantities vary according to family traditions. You can prepare ndolé with meat, fish, seafood, or a combination of these.*

## 4. Preparation Instructions

### Step 1: Prepare the Bitter Leaves

1. Wash the bitter leaves thoroughly.
2. If using fresh leaves, boil them briefly if necessary, then wash and squeeze them repeatedly to reduce bitterness.
3. Taste a small amount and repeat the washing process if the leaves are still excessively bitter.
4. Drain the leaves thoroughly and set them aside.

**Tip:** Traditional ndolé has a slight bitter taste, but the bitterness should not overpower the groundnut sauce.

### Step 2: Cook the Meat

1. Wash the beef and cow skin thoroughly with clean water.
2. Cut them into manageable pieces.
3. Place them in a pot with salt, some chopped onion, and seasoning cubes if desired.
4. Add enough water to cook the meat.
5. Cover and simmer until the meat becomes tender.
6. Reserve the meat stock for preparing the ndolé.

### Step 3: Prepare the Groundnuts

1. Remove any stones or unwanted particles from the raw groundnuts.
2. Rinse them thoroughly.
3. Place them in a pot, cover with water, and boil until softened.
4. Drain and allow them to cool slightly.
5. Grind the cooked groundnuts with a blender or grinding machine until they form a smooth or slightly coarse paste.

**Tip:** Avoid adding too much water while grinding. A thick groundnut paste helps produce a rich sauce.

### Step 4: Prepare the Other Ingredients

1. Peel and chop the onions.
2. Peel and crush the garlic.
3. Peel and grate or crush the ginger.
4. Clean the smoked fish, removing bones where possible.
5. Clean the prawns if using them.
6. Grind the crayfish and other dry spices.

### Step 5: Cook the Groundnut Paste

1. Place the groundnut paste in a large cooking pot.
2. Add some of the reserved meat stock and stir until the paste reaches a thick but manageable consistency.
3. Place the pot over medium heat.
4. Stir regularly to prevent the paste from sticking to the bottom.
5. Allow it to simmer for approximately 15–25 minutes, adding small amounts of water as necessary.

The groundnut mixture should cook thoroughly and begin to develop a rich aroma.

### Step 6: Add the Meat and Fish

1. Add the cooked beef and cow skin to the groundnut mixture.
2. Add the smoked fish and ground crayfish.
3. Add the chopped onions, garlic, ginger, and other spices.
4. Stir gently to combine the ingredients.
5. Allow the mixture to simmer for about 10–15 minutes.

### Step 7: Add the Bitter Leaves

1. Add the prepared bitter leaves to the pot.
2. Mix thoroughly so that the leaves are evenly distributed throughout the groundnut sauce.
3. Add the prawns if using them.
4. Allow everything to simmer for another 10–15 minutes, or until the leaves and seafood are properly cooked.
5. Add salt and hot pepper to taste.

### Step 8: Add the Palm Oil

1. Heat the red palm oil gently in a separate pan.
2. Add the remaining chopped onion and fry briefly if desired.
3. Pour the palm oil into the ndolé.
4. Stir gently to distribute the oil evenly.
5. Allow the dish to simmer for another 5–10 minutes.

The finished ndolé should have a rich, thick consistency, with the bitter leaves evenly mixed into the groundnut sauce.

**Important:** Do not allow the sauce to become too dry or stick to the bottom of the pot. Add a little hot water if necessary and continue cooking gently.

## 5. Tips for Preparing Delicious Ndolé

- **Reduce excessive bitterness:** Wash and squeeze fresh bitter leaves thoroughly.
- **Cook the groundnuts properly:** Undercooked groundnuts can affect the flavour and texture of the dish.
- **Use good-quality palm oil:** It contributes to the traditional colour and flavour.
- **Add smoked fish and crayfish:** These ingredients provide a distinctive savoury taste.
- **Avoid excessive water:** A thick, creamy groundnut sauce is generally preferred.
- **Stir regularly:** Groundnut paste can burn easily, especially at the bottom of the pot.
- **Balance the flavours:** Adjust salt, pepper, and bitterness to your preference.

## 6. What to Serve with Ndolé

Ndolé is commonly served with:

- Boiled ripe or unripe plantains
- Bobolo
- Miondo
- Boiled cassava
- Fried ripe plantains
- Boiled rice

For a traditional Cameroonian meal, serve ndolé with bobolo or boiled plantains.

## 7. Storage

- Allow the ndolé to cool slightly before storing it in covered containers.
- Refrigerate promptly, ideally within two hours of cooking.
- Refrigerate leftovers for approximately 3–4 days.
- Freeze portions for longer storage.
- Reheat thoroughly before serving, adding a little water if the sauce becomes too thick.

## 8. Conclusion

Ndolé is a rich and flavourful Cameroonian dish made with bitter leaves, groundnuts, palm oil, and a combination of meat, fish, or seafood.

The secret to delicious ndolé is properly prepared bitter leaves, thoroughly cooked groundnuts, well-seasoned meat stock, and slow cooking to allow the flavours to blend together.
