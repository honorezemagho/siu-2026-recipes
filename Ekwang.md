# Traditional Cameroonian Ekwang Recipe

## 1. Introduction

Ekwang is a traditional Cameroonian dish, particularly popular in the Southwest and Littoral Regions. It is made from grated cocoyam wrapped in cocoyam leaves, carefully arranged in a pot, and slowly cooked with palm oil, spices, and assorted meat or fish.

Ekwang is known for its rich flavour, soft texture, and delicious palm oil sauce.

## 2. Preparation Time

- **Preparation time:** 45–60 minutes
- **Cooking time:** 1½–2 hours
- **Total time:** Approximately 2½–3 hours
- **Servings:** 4–6 people

## 3. Ingredients

### A. For the Ekwang

- 2 kg cocoyam (taro)
- 1–2 bunches of cocoyam leaves
- 250–350 ml red palm oil
- 500 g beef
- 250 g smoked fish or dried fish
- 250 g cow skin (canda), optional
- 1 medium onion, optional
- 2–3 cloves garlic, optional
- 1–2 seasoning cubes, optional
- Salt to taste
- Water as needed

### B. For the Spices

- 1 teaspoon ground country onion, optional
- 1 teaspoon ground crayfish
- 1 teaspoon white pepper, optional
- 1 teaspoon ground ginger, optional
- Fresh or dried hot pepper, to taste

*Note: Ingredients and quantities vary according to family traditions and personal preferences.*

## 4. Preparation Instructions

### Step 1: Prepare the Meat and Fish

1. Wash the beef and cow skin thoroughly with clean water.
2. Cut the meat into manageable pieces.
3. Put the meat in a pot and season with salt, onion, garlic, and seasoning cubes if desired.
4. Add a little water and cook until the meat is nearly tender.
5. Clean the smoked or dried fish carefully, removing bones where possible.
6. Set the cooked meat and stock aside.

### Step 2: Prepare the Cocoyam

1. Peel the cocoyam.
2. Wash it thoroughly.
3. Grate the cocoyam into a smooth, thick paste using a grater.
4. Set the grated cocoyam aside in a clean bowl.

**Tip:** Grate the cocoyam finely to achieve a smooth texture after cooking.

### Step 3: Prepare the Cocoyam Leaves

1. Wash the cocoyam leaves thoroughly.
2. Remove the tough central veins if necessary.
3. Cut large leaves into manageable pieces.
4. Set the leaves aside.

**Important:** Use leaves suitable for traditional Ekwang preparation. Some cocoyam leaves can cause irritation if improperly prepared, so cook the dish thoroughly.

### Step 4: Wrap the Grated Cocoyam

1. Place a small quantity of grated cocoyam on the inner side of a cocoyam leaf.
2. Spread it into a narrow strip.
3. Roll the leaf tightly around the cocoyam paste.
4. Repeat until all the paste has been wrapped.

### Step 5: Arrange the Ekwang in the Pot

1. Lightly grease the bottom of a large cooking pot with palm oil.
2. Arrange the wrapped cocoyam pieces tightly in the pot, one beside another.
3. Continue layering until all the pieces are arranged.
4. Add the cooked meat, cow skin, and smoked fish between the layers or on top.

**Tip:** Arrange the wraps carefully to prevent them from unrolling during cooking.

### Step 6: Add the Palm Oil and Spices

1. Pour the red palm oil over the arranged Ekwang.
2. Add the ground crayfish, country onion, white pepper, ginger, and hot pepper if using.
3. Add the reserved meat stock.
4. Pour in enough water to help the Ekwang cook without completely submerging it.
5. Add salt to taste.

### Step 7: Cook the Ekwang

1. Cover the pot and place it over medium heat.
2. Allow it to cook gently for approximately 1½–2 hours.
3. Check periodically to ensure there is enough liquid at the bottom.
4. If necessary, add small amounts of hot water around the edges of the pot.
5. Avoid stirring vigorously, as this can break the cocoyam wraps.
6. When the cocoyam is thoroughly cooked, the wraps should be soft and the sauce should be rich and well blended.

Cooking time depends on the quantity prepared, the thickness of the wraps, and the type of cocoyam used.

## 5. Tips for Delicious Ekwang

- **Use fresh cocoyam:** Fresh, firm cocoyam produces a good texture.
- **Grate finely:** Fine grating helps the cocoyam cook evenly.
- **Wrap tightly:** Secure wrapping helps prevent the paste from escaping.
- **Use enough palm oil:** Palm oil contributes to Ekwang's characteristic colour and flavour.
- **Cook slowly:** Gentle cooking helps the wraps hold their shape.
- **Avoid excessive stirring:** Move the pot gently if you need to redistribute the liquid.
- **Season to taste:** Smoked fish and crayfish add depth to the flavour.

## 6. Serving Suggestions

Ekwang can be served on its own as a complete meal or accompanied by your preferred traditional side dish.

It is especially delicious when prepared with:
- Smoked fish
- Beef
- Cow skin (canda)
- Crayfish
- Traditional spices and palm oil

## 7. Storage

- Allow leftovers to cool slightly before refrigerating them in covered containers.
- Refrigerate promptly, ideally within two hours of cooking.
- Reheat thoroughly before serving.
- Do not leave cooked Ekwang at room temperature for extended periods.

## 8. Conclusion

Ekwang is a delicious traditional Cameroonian dish made with grated cocoyam wrapped in cocoyam leaves and slowly cooked in palm oil with meat, fish, and spices.

The secret to excellent Ekwang is finely grated cocoyam, tightly wrapped leaves, balanced seasoning, and slow cooking until the cocoyam is completely cooked and the flavours have blended together.
