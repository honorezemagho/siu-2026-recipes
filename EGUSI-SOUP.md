# Traditional Cameroonian Egusi Recipe

## 1. Introduction

Egusi is a popular traditional dish in Cameroon and other parts of West and Central Africa. It is prepared using ground melon seeds, leafy vegetables, palm oil, and ingredients such as meat, fish, and crayfish.

Cameroonian egusi is known for its rich flavour, thick consistency, and delicious combination of spices and vegetables. Depending on the region and family tradition, it may be prepared with spinach, bitter leaves, waterleaf, or other local greens.

## 2. Preparation Time

- **Preparation time:** 30–45 minutes
- **Cooking time:** 45–60 minutes
- **Total time:** Approximately 1½ hours
- **Servings:** 4–6 people

## 3. Ingredients

### A. Main Ingredients

- 500 g ground egusi (melon seeds)
- 500 g beef
- 250 g smoked fish or dried fish
- 250 g cow skin (canda), optional
- 2 bunches of spinach, waterleaf, or another suitable leafy vegetable
- 150–250 ml red palm oil
- 2 medium onions
- 2–3 cloves garlic
- 1 piece of ginger, approximately 2–3 cm
- 2 tablespoons ground crayfish
- 1–2 seasoning cubes, optional
- Salt to taste
- Fresh hot pepper, to taste
- Water as needed

### B. Optional Ingredients

- 250 g prawns or shrimp
- 200 g stockfish, soaked and cleaned
- 1 teaspoon ground white pepper
- 1 teaspoon country onion
- A small amount of bitter leaf for a more traditional flavour

*Note: Ingredients vary according to personal preference and regional cooking traditions.*

## 4. Preparation Instructions

### Step 1: Prepare the Meat

1. Wash the beef and cow skin thoroughly with clean water.
2. Cut them into manageable pieces.
3. Place the meat in a pot.
4. Add salt, some chopped onion, garlic, and seasoning cubes if desired.
5. Add a small amount of water.
6. Cover and cook until the meat is tender.
7. Reserve the meat stock for preparing the egusi.

### Step 2: Prepare the Egusi Seeds

1. Remove any stones or unwanted particles from the egusi seeds.
2. Grind the seeds into a fine or slightly coarse powder.
3. Place the ground egusi in a bowl.
4. Add a small amount of water and mix into a thick paste.

**Tip:** Add water gradually. The mixture should be thick rather than watery.

### Step 3: Prepare the Vegetables

1. Remove any damaged leaves from the vegetables.
2. Wash them thoroughly under clean running water.
3. Drain the vegetables.
4. Chop them into small pieces.
5. Set them aside.

If you are using bitter leaves, wash and squeeze them as needed to reduce excessive bitterness.

### Step 4: Prepare the Fish and Spices

1. Clean the smoked or dried fish carefully.
2. Remove the bones where possible.
3. If using stockfish, soak and cook it until tender.
4. Peel and chop the remaining onions.
5. Crush the garlic and ginger.
6. Grind the crayfish and other dry spices.

### Step 5: Heat the Palm Oil

1. Pour the red palm oil into a large cooking pot.
2. Heat it gently over medium heat.
3. Add the chopped onions.
4. Fry briefly until fragrant.

**Important:** Avoid overheating the palm oil or allowing it to smoke excessively.

### Step 6: Cook the Egusi

1. Add the ground egusi paste to the pot.
2. Stir gently to combine it with the palm oil.
3. Allow it to cook for approximately 5–10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
4. Add some reserved meat stock gradually.
5. Stir to prevent the egusi from sticking to the bottom of the pot.
6. Cover and simmer for another 10–15 minutes.

The egusi should begin to thicken and develop a rich, savoury flavour.

### Step 7: Add the Meat and Fish

1. Add the cooked beef and cow skin.
2. Add the smoked fish, stockfish, or prawns if using.
3. Add the ground crayfish, garlic, ginger, and other spices.
4. Add hot pepper according to your taste.
5. Stir gently to combine the ingredients.
6. Allow the mixture to simmer for approximately 10 minutes.

Add small amounts of water or stock if the mixture becomes too thick.

### Step 8: Add the Vegetables

1. Add the chopped vegetables to the pot.
2. Stir thoroughly so that the vegetables are evenly distributed.
3. Cover and simmer until the vegetables are tender.
4. Depending on the vegetables used, this may take approximately 5–10 minutes.
5. Taste the sauce and adjust the salt and seasoning.

Avoid overcooking delicate leafy vegetables, as they can lose their colour and texture.

### Step 9: Final Cooking

1. Allow the egusi to simmer gently for another 5 minutes.
2. Check that the egusi, meat, fish, and vegetables are thoroughly cooked.
3. Adjust the consistency with a little hot water if necessary.
4. Remove the pot from the heat and allow it to rest briefly before serving.

The finished egusi should be thick, flavourful, and well combined, with the palm oil, ground seeds, vegetables, and protein forming a rich sauce.

## 5. Tips for Delicious Egusi

- **Use fresh egusi seeds:** Fresh seeds provide a better flavour and aroma.
- **Grind the seeds properly:** A fine or moderately coarse grind helps produce a consistent sauce.
- **Do not add too much water at once:** Gradual additions help control the thickness.
- **Use well-seasoned meat stock:** It adds depth and richness to the dish.
- **Add crayfish and smoked fish:** These ingredients contribute a distinctive traditional flavour.
- **Cook over moderate heat:** This helps prevent the egusi from burning.
- **Choose suitable vegetables:** Spinach, waterleaf, and bitter leaf each produce a different result.
- **Adjust palm oil to taste:** The quantity depends on your preferred richness and the amount of egusi used.

## 6. What to Serve with Egusi

Egusi can be served with several traditional Cameroonian accompaniments, including:

- Boiled ripe or unripe plantains
- Boiled cassava
- Bobolo
- Miondo
- Fufu made from cassava
- Corn fufu
- Garri prepared as a dough
- Boiled rice

For a traditional Cameroonian meal, serve egusi with boiled plantains, cassava fufu, or corn fufu.

## 7. Storage

- Allow the egusi to cool slightly before transferring it to covered containers.
- Refrigerate promptly, ideally within two hours of cooking.
- Store refrigerated leftovers for approximately 3–4 days.
- Freeze portions for longer storage.
- Reheat thoroughly before serving, adding a little water if the sauce has become too thick.

## 8. Conclusion

Cameroonian egusi is a delicious and nourishing dish made with ground melon seeds, palm oil, leafy vegetables, meat, fish, and traditional spices.

The secret to excellent egusi is properly ground seeds, well-seasoned stock, balanced palm oil, fresh vegetables, and gentle cooking to allow the flavours to blend together.
